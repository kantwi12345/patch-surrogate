# M4 Results: Real HFSS Simulation vs. Analytical Surrogate Ground Truth

## Summary

8 designs spanning 1.2-5.8 GHz, generated by `invert.py`'s `design_from_spec()`
(the same M3 inversion pipeline), were simulated with full-wave HFSS
(`simulate_hfss()`, driven-modal, pyaedt/AEDT 2022 R1) and compared against
the analytical cavity-model predictions (`simulate()`) used as ground truth
during M1-M3 development.

**Headline finding: the analytical model's resonant-frequency prediction is
reasonably accurate (2.7-7.1% error vs. HFSS), but its feed-matching
(inset-depth) prediction is catastrophically and universally wrong — all 8
of 8 designs show severe impedance mismatch in HFSS that the analytical
model did not predict at all.**

## Interpretation

### 1. Resonant frequency: modest, systematic divergence
HFSS resonant frequency is consistently **lower** than the analytical
prediction by 2.7-7.1% across every design, with no obvious dependence on
substrate or target frequency. This is consistent with known limitations of
the closed-form fringing-field correction (`_eps_reff_and_deltaL`) used in
the cavity model -- a well-understood, bounded source of error, not a bug.

### 2. Impedance matching: severe, universal divergence
Every single design shows dramatically worse S11 in HFSS than predicted,
with peak gain crushed correspondingly (mismatch loss directly eats into
realized gain). The degradation is total and shows no correlation with
substrate permittivity, patch size, or frequency -- it affects FR4, Alumina,
RO4003C, RO3003, and RT/duroid5880 designs equally.

**Root cause**: `find_matched_inset()`'s closed-form two-slot cavity model
for computing the 50-ohm-matching inset depth `y0` systematically
overshoots the true full-wave match point. This was independently confirmed
via manual bisection calibration on a held-out test design
(`L=29.40mm, W=38.00mm, h=1.6mm, eps_r=4.40`): the analytical model predicted
`y0=10.90mm` (giving a pathological Re(Zin)=3.36 ohm, -0.99dB match), while
the true HFSS-confirmed match point was `y0=7.20mm` (Re(Zin)=56.97 ohm,
-23.52dB match) -- more than 3mm of systematic overshoot on a ~38mm-wide
patch.

### 3. A note on methodology: resonance-detection fix
An earlier pass of this validation set (preserved in
`data/m4_validation_results_BUGGY_BACKUP.json`) showed 4 of 8 designs with
HFSS resonance pegged exactly at the sweep's edge, even after widening the
sweep window 2.5x. Root-causing this revealed it was NOT a new bug, but a
visualization of the SAME matching failure described above, combined with a
naive global-argmin resonance detector: for designs where the true (but
severely mismatched, shallow) cavity resonance dip is much shallower than
unrelated broadband feed-discontinuity reflection that grows toward the
sweep edge, global argmin(S11) picks the edge, not the real dip. This was
fixed by adding `_find_resonance()`, which restricts the search to genuine
interior local minima nearest the analytical cavity-mode frequency estimate
`fr_est` (which depends only on L/W/h/eps_r, not on the feed position, so it
remains a valid, matching-independent physical prior). All 8 designs in the
table above use the corrected detector.

## Decision for M5

Given:
- A **bounded, systematic, well-understood** ~2.7-7.1% frequency-prediction
  error (acceptable; a surrogate trained on analytical data would still
  roughly localize the right frequency band), combined with
- A **severe, universal, 100%-reproducible** failure of the analytical
  model's impedance-matching prediction (completely unacceptable; a
  surrogate trained purely on analytical data would confidently predict
  excellent matches that do not exist in reality for ANY design in this
  sample),

the analytical model is **not sufficient as a standalone ground truth** for
a deployable inverse-design tool. The core patch dimensions (L, W) it
produces are usable as a starting point / prior, but the predicted feed
inset depth (y0) cannot be trusted at all.

**Recommendation for M5**: adopt an active-learning loop that mixes in
HFSS-labeled data specifically targeting the y0/matching relationship
(e.g. sampling y0 densely at fixed L/W/h/eps_r and learning the true
Rin(y0) curve from HFSS, rather than trusting the closed-form formula), while
continuing to use the analytical model's L/W sizing (frequency prediction)
as a cheap prior to keep the HFSS sample budget small.
